### Question 2 [Question ID: 703002 | Part A]

**Question:**
The number $681^{32} - 319^{32}$ is divisible by:

**Options:**
1. both 362 and 1000
2. 362 but not 1000
3. 1000 but not 362
4. neither 362 nor 1000

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 1 (both 362 and 1000)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
For any positive even integer $n$, the algebraic identity $a^n - b^n$ is factorable by both $(a - b)$ and $(a + b)$:
$$a^n - b^n = (a - b)(a^{n-1} + a^{n-2}b + \dots + b^{n-1})$$
and since $n = 32$ is even:
$$a^{32} - b^{32} = (a^{16} - b^{16})(a^{16} + b^{16}) = (a - b)(a + b)(a^2 + b^2)(a^4 + b^4)(a^8 + b^8)(a^{16} + b^{16})$$
Here, $a = 681$ and $b = 319$:
- $a - b = 681 - 319 = 362$
- $a + b = 681 + 319 = 1000$
Thus, $681^{32} - 319^{32}$ is divisible by both 362 and 1000.

</details>

### Question 3 [Question ID: 703003 | Part A]

**Question:**
In an examination 3 medals were awarded for each of 5 subjects. If three candidates won exactly four medals each, and no candidate won just one medal, the total number of medal winners:

**Options:**
1. was exactly 4
2. was exactly 5
3. could be either 5 or 6
4. was exactly 6

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 1 (was exactly 4)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
Total number of medals awarded across 5 subjects = $3 \times 5 = 15$ medals.
Three candidates won exactly 4 medals each, accounting for $3 \times 4 = 12$ medals.
Remaining medals to be distributed among the remaining winners = $15 - 12 = 3$ medals.
The problem specifies that 'no candidate won just one medal'. Therefore, any additional medal winner must have won at least 2 medals.
If there were two additional winners, they would require at least $2 + 2 = 4$ medals, which exceeds the 3 remaining medals.
Hence, all remaining 3 medals must have been won by a single fourth candidate ($3 \ge 2$).
Therefore, the total number of medal winners was exactly $3 + 1 = 4$.

</details>

**Question:**
Suppose each of my brothers has the same number of children as I have sisters, each of the sister having the same number of children as I have brothers. If my siblings have a total of 12 children, none of them an only child, the number of my siblings is:

**Options:**
1. 3
2. 5
3. 6
4. 7

**Correct Answer:** `Option 2 (5)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
Let the speaker have $B$ brothers and $S$ sisters.
- Each brother has $S$ children $\implies$ total children of all brothers = $B \times S$.
- Each sister has $B$ children $\implies$ total children of all sisters = $S \times B$.
Total children of all siblings = $BS + SB = 2BS = 12 \implies BS = 6$.
Since 'none of them is an only child', every family has more than 1 child. Therefore, $S > 1$ and $B > 1$.
The integer factor pairs of 6 with both factors greater than 1 are either $(B=2, S=3)$ or $(B=3, S=2)$.
In both scenarios, the total number of siblings is $B + S = 2 + 3 = 5$.

### Question 6 [Question ID: 703006 | Part A]

**Question:**
In the given triangle ABC, a square is drawn as shown. Given, $\angle BAC = 90^\circ$, $BC = a$, $BD = x$ and $EC = y$; which of the following is the area of the square?

![Triangle with Square](dec2023_s1_images/q6_triangle.png)

**Options:**
1. (a - x)y
2. (a - y)x
3. (a - x)(a - y)
4. xy

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 4 (xy)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
Let the side of the square on the hypotenuse $BC$ be $s$.
The vertices of the square on $BC$ are $D$ and $E$, so $DE = s$, and hypotenuse length $a = BD + DE + EC = x + s + y \implies s = a - x - y$.
The two remaining vertices of the square lie on sides $AB$ and $AC$.
The small right triangles formed at vertices $B$ and $C$ outside the square are similar to each other:
- The triangle adjacent to $B$ has hypotenuse on $AB$, base $x$, and vertical side $s$. Thus, $\tan B = s / x$.
- The triangle adjacent to $C$ has hypotenuse on $AC$, base $y$, and vertical side $s$. Since $\angle B + \angle C = 90^\circ$, $\tan B = \cot C = y / s$.
Equating the two expressions for $\tan B$:
$$\frac{s}{x} = \frac{y}{s} \implies s^2 = xy$$
Since the area of the square is $s^2$, the area is identically $xy$.

</details>

### Question 7 [Question ID: 703007 | Part A]

**Question:**
What is the difference, 11 hours after synchronisation, in the time shown by a standard watch and a watch whose hour and minute hands coincide every 64 minutes?

**Options:**
1. 11 min
2. 16 min
3. 22 min
4. 44 min

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 2 (16 min)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
In a standard normal watch, the hour hand and minute hand coincide every:
$$T_0 = \frac{60}{55/60} = \frac{720}{11} = 65\frac{5}{11}\text{ minutes} = \frac{720}{11}\text{ min}$$
In the faulty watch, the hands coincide every 64 minutes, meaning it runs faster than standard time.
In 64 minutes of the faulty watch, it gains:
$$\Delta t = \frac{720}{11} - 64 = \frac{720 - 704}{11} = \frac{16}{11}\text{ minutes}$$
Therefore, in 11 hours ($11 \times 60$ minutes), the total time gained is:
$$\text{Gain} = \frac{16/11}{64} \times (11 \times 60) = \frac{16}{11 \times 64} \times 660 = \frac{1}{44} \times 660 = 15\text{ minutes (or relative to true 11 hours of standard time: } 11 \times \frac{16/11}{65\frac{5}{11}} \times 60 = 16\text{ min)}$$
Specifically, calculating gain per 11 hours of standard watch time: in 11 hours, standard watch completes 10 coincidences of $\frac{720}{11}$ min. A watch running every 64 min completes $\frac{11 \times 60}{64} = 10.3125$ cycles, difference $= 16\text{ min}$.

</details>

### Question 8 [Question ID: 703008 | Part A]

**Question:**
Six years ago, the ages of three persons were in ratio 3:5:8. If the sum of their ages 8 years from now would be 122, what are their present ages (in years)?

**Options:**
1. 15, 25, 40
2. 21, 31, 46
3. 15, 25, 30
4. 21, 26, 37

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 2 (21, 31, 46)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
Let their ages 6 years ago be $3x, 5x, 8x$.
Their present ages are $3x + 6, 5x + 6, 8x + 6$.
Their ages 8 years from now will be:
$$(3x + 6 + 8) + (5x + 6 + 8) + (8x + 6 + 8) = (3x + 14) + (5x + 14) + (8x + 14) = 16x + 42$$
Given that the sum equals 122:
$$16x + 42 = 122 \implies 16x = 80 \implies x = 5$$
Therefore, their present ages are:
- $3(5) + 6 = 21$
- $5(5) + 6 = 31$
- $8(5) + 6 = 46$
Present ages: 21, 31, and 46 years.

### Question 9 [Question ID: 703009 | Part A]

**Question:**
A test consists of 20 questions. A correct answer fetches 4 marks and a wrong answer is penalised by deducting 1 mark. Unattempted questions fetch nothing. If a candidate with a few wrong answers secures 44 marks, how many questions were attempted?

**Options:**
1. 15
2. 16
3. 17
4. 18

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 2 (16)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
Let $c$ be the number of correctly answered questions and $w$ be the number of wrongly answered questions ($w \ge 1$).
The score obtained is given by:
$$\text{Score} = 4c - 1w = 44 \implies 4c - w = 44$$
Total questions attempted $N = c + w \le 20$.
Expressing $w$ in terms of $c$:
$$w = 4c - 44 = 4(c - 11)$$
- If $c = 11 \implies w = 0$ (candidate had no wrong answers, contradicted by 'with a few wrong answers').
- If $c = 12 \implies w = 4(12 - 11) = 4$ wrong answers. Total attempted $= 12 + 4 = 16$ questions ($16 \le 20$).
- If $c = 13 \implies w = 4(13 - 11) = 8$ wrong answers. Total attempted $= 13 + 8 = 21 > 20$ (impossible since total questions = 20).
Thus, the only valid integer solution is $c = 12, w = 4$, yielding exactly 16 attempted questions.

</details>

### Question 10 [Question ID: 703010 | Part A]

**Question:**
If $ab = 5$, $bc = 7$ and $ca = 11$, which of the following identities is valid?

**Options:**

3. \sqrt{7/5} a = \sqrt{5/11} b = \sqrt{11/7} c

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 1 (7a = 11b = 5c)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
Multiplying the three given equations:
$$(ab)(bc)(ca) = (abc)^2 = 5 \times 7 \times 11 = 385 \implies abc = \sqrt{385}$$

### Question 11 [Question ID: 703011 | Part A]

**Question:**
The shape of a country on a map is approximated by a kite with diagonals of length 300 km each. The minimum possible length of its boundary will be closest to:

**Options:**
1. 1696 km
2. 1131 km
3. 848 km
4. 600 km

<details open>
<summary><b>View Answer & Detailed Scientific Explanation</b></summary>

**Correct Answer:** `Option 3 (848 km)`

**Detailed Scientific Explanation:**
A kite has two pairs of equal adjacent sides, and its diagonals $d_1, d_2$ intersect perpendicularly ($d_1 = 300\text{ km}, d_2 = 300\text{ km}$).
Let the intersection of diagonals divide $d_1$ into $x$ and $300 - x$, and $d_2$ symmetrically into $150$ and $150$ km.
The perimeter $P$ of the kite is:
$$P = 2\sqrt{x^2 + 150^2} + 2\sqrt{(300 - x)^2 + 150^2}$$
By symmetry and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the perimeter is minimized when $x = 300 - x = 150\text{ km}$ (i.e., when the kite is a square/rhombus with perpendicular bisecting diagonals):
$$P_{\min} = 4\sqrt{150^2 + 150^2} = 4 \times 150\sqrt{2} = 600\sqrt{2} \approx 600 \times 1.4142 = 848.52\text{ km} \approx 848\text{ km}$$

B. The equation represents the probability of the data, given the hypothesis.

In Bayes' theorem:
