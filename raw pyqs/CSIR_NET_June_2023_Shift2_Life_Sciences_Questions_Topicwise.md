### Question 1 (QID: 703501 | Part A)

**Question:**
A certain number of apples, when attempted to be distributed to three groups in the ratio 3:4:5, fall short by one. If distributed in the ratio 4:5:6 two apples would be left undistributed. Which one of the following is a possible number of apples?

**Options:**
- 1. 35
- 2. 47
- 3. 59
- 4. 62

**Official Answer:**
**Option 2 (47)**

**Explanation:**
Let $N$ be the total number of apples.

1. **First Condition (Ratio 3:4:5):**
   - The sum of ratio terms is $3 + 4 + 5 = 12$.
   - Since the apples fall short by 1 when divided into this ratio, adding 1 apple makes the total exactly divisible by 12:
     $$N + 1 \equiv 0 \pmod{12} \implies N \equiv -1 \equiv 11 \pmod{12}$$

2. **Second Condition (Ratio 4:5:6):**
   - The sum of ratio terms is $4 + 5 + 6 = 15$.
   - When distributed in this ratio, 2 apples are left over, meaning subtracting 2 makes the total divisible by 15:
     $$N - 2 \equiv 0 \pmod{15} \implies N \equiv 2 \pmod{15}$$

3. **Testing the Given Options:**
   - **Option 1 (35):** $35 + 1 = 36$ (divisible by 12); but $35 - 2 = 33$ (not divisible by 15).
   - **Option 2 (47):** $47 + 1 = 48$ ($48/12 = 4$, divisible by 12); and $47 - 2 = 45$ ($45/15 = 3$, divisible by 15). Both conditions are satisfied!
   - **Option 3 (59):** $59 + 1 = 60$ (divisible by 12); but $59 - 2 = 57$ (not divisible by 15).
   - **Option 4 (62):** $62 + 1 = 63$ (not divisible by 12).

Therefore, the possible number of apples is **47** (Option 2).

### Question 2 (QID: 703502 | Part A)

**Question:**
In a queue, A stands 14th from the front and B 17th from the last. C is exactly at the middle of the queue with 16 persons between him and A. How many persons are between B and C?

**Options:**
- 1. 11
- 2. 12
- 3. 13
- 4. 14

**Official Answer:**
**Option 3 (13)**

**Explanation:**
Let us determine the positions of persons in the queue from the front:

- A is 14th from the front $\implies \text{Position}(A) = 14$.
- C is positioned behind A with 16 persons between them:
  $$\text{Position}(C) = 14 + 16 + 1 = 31$$
- C is situated exactly in the middle of the entire queue. Therefore, there are 30 persons in front of C and 30 persons behind C:
  $$\text{Total persons in queue } N = 30 + 1 + 30 = 61$$
- B stands 17th from the last position. The position of B from the front is:
  $$\text{Position}(B) = N - 17 + 1 = 61 - 17 + 1 = 45$$

The number of persons standing between C (position 31) and B (position 45) is:
$$\text{Persons between } B \text{ and } C = 45 - 31 - 1 = 13$$

Therefore, there are **13 persons** between B and C (Option 3).

### Question 3 (QID: 703503 | Part A)

**Question:**
Capital investments of two partners, A and B, are different. A gets 20% of the profit as bonus, and the rest of the profit is distributed in the proportion of their investment. If the entire profit were divided in proportion to their capital investment, B would have got Rs. 25,000 more than he gets now. What amount does B get now?

**Options:**
- 1. 2.00 lakh
- 2. 1.50 lakh
- 3. 1.25 lakh
- 4. 1.00 lakh

**Official Answer:**
**Option 4 (1.00 lakh)**

**Explanation:**
Let $P$ be the total profit, and let $f_B$ be the fraction of total capital invested by partner B ($0 < f_B < 1$).

1. **Current Distribution Scheme:**
   - A takes 20% of the profit as a bonus ($0.20 P$).
   - The remaining 80% of profit ($0.80 P$) is distributed according to capital investment.
   - The amount B receives currently is:
     $$B_{\text{now}} = 0.80 P \times f_B$$

2. **Alternative Proportional Scheme:**
   - If the entire profit $P$ were divided solely in proportion to capital investment, B would receive:
     $$B_{\text{prop}} = P \times f_B$$

3. **Difference in B's Share:**
   $$B_{\text{prop}} - B_{\text{now}} = P f_B - 0.80 P f_B = 0.20 P f_B = 25,000$$
   $$\implies P f_B = \frac{25,000}{0.20} = 125,000\text{ Rs. (1.25 lakh)}$$

4. **Amount B Receives Now:**
   $$B_{\text{now}} = 0.80 \times (P f_B) = 0.80 \times 125,000 = 100,000\text{ Rs.} = \mathbf{1.00\text{ lakh}}$$

Therefore, the amount B receives currently is **Rs. 1.00 lakh** (Option 4).

### Question 4 (QID: 703504 | Part A)

**Question:**
Given that $a \ne b$ and $a, b > 0$, and $\frac{a^{n+2} + b^{n+2}}{a^n + b^n} = ab$, then $n =$

**Options:**
- 1. 1
- 2. 1/2
- 3. -1/2
- 4. -1

**Official Answer:**
**Option 4 (-1)**

**Explanation:**
Given the equation:
$$\frac{a^{n+2} + b^{n+2}}{a^n + b^n} = ab$$

Cross-multiplying:
$$a^{n+2} + b^{n+2} = ab(a^n + b^n)$$
$$a^{n+2} + b^{n+2} = a^{n+1}b + ab^{n+1}$$

Rearranging like terms to one side:
$$a^{n+2} - a^{n+1}b = ab^{n+1} - b^{n+2}$$
$$a^{n+1}(a - b) = b^{n+1}(a - b)$$

Since $a \ne b$, we have $(a - b) \ne 0$. Dividing both sides by $(a - b)$:
$$a^{n+1} = b^{n+1} \implies \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{n+1} = 1$$

Since $a \ne b$, $\frac{a}{b} \ne 1$. For any positive base other than 1, $(a/b)^x = 1$ implies $x = 0$:
$$n + 1 = 0 \implies n = -1$$

Therefore, $n = \mathbf{-1}$ (Option 4).

### Question 5 (QID: 703505 | Part A)

**Question:**
To complete a task, A takes 15 days whereas B takes 21 days. If they work alone on alternate days by turn, B starting on the first day, the number of days required to complete the task is

**Options:**
- 1. 8 3/35
- 2. 16 3/35
- 3. 18 2/5
- 4. 17 4/7

**Official Answer:**
**Option 4 (17 4/7)**

**Explanation:**
Let total work be $\text{LCM}(15, 21) = 105$ units.

- Rate of work of A $= \frac{105}{15} = 7$ units/day.
- Rate of work of B $= \frac{105}{21} = 5$ units/day.

They work alternately, with B starting on Day 1:
- Day 1 (B): 5 units
- Day 2 (A): 7 units
- Work done in one 2-day cycle $= 5 + 7 = 12$ units.

Number of complete 2-day cycles:
$$8 \text{ cycles} \times 12\text{ units/cycle} = 96\text{ units in } 16\text{ days}$$

Remaining work after 16 days:
$$105 - 96 = 9\text{ units}$$

- Day 17: B works and completes 5 units $\implies$ Remaining work $= 9 - 5 = 4$ units.
- Day 18: A works. A does 7 units/day, so the time taken to complete 4 units is $\frac{4}{7}$ day.

Total time taken $= 17 + \frac{4}{7} = \mathbf{17\frac{4}{7}\text{ days}}$ (Option 4).

### Question 6 (QID: 703506 | Part A)

**Question:**
Two fair cubical dice are thrown. What is the probability that at least one of them shows 4?

**Options:**
- 1. 1/36
- 2. 11/36
- 3. 1/6
- 4. 1/18

**Official Answer:**
**Option 2 (11/36)**

**Explanation:**
When two fair 6-sided dice are thrown, the total number of equally likely elementary outcomes is:
$$N(\text{Total}) = 6 \times 6 = 36$$

Let $E$ be the event that at least one die shows 4. Its complementary event $E'$ is that neither die shows 4.
Each die has 5 faces that are not 4 (namely {1, 2, 3, 5, 6}):
$$N(E') = 5 \times 5 = 25$$

Therefore, the number of favorable outcomes for at least one 4 is:
$$N(E) = 36 - 25 = 11$$

The required probability is:
$$P(E) = \frac{11}{36}$$

Therefore, the probability is **11/36** (Option 2).

### Question 7 (QID: 703507 | Part A)

**Question:**
Straight lines drawn inside a rectangle can create multiple rectangles of different sizes. The lines can intersect each other. The minimum number of straight lines needed so as to result in a total of 9 rectangles is

**Options:**
- 1. 5
- 2. 2
- 3. 3
- 4. 4

**Official Answer:**
**Option 2 (2)**

**Explanation:**
Consider a rectangle. If we draw $h$ horizontal lines and $v$ vertical lines inside it parallel to its edges:
- The total number of horizontal boundary lines (including the top and bottom edges of the rectangle) is $h + 2$.
- The total number of vertical boundary lines (including the left and right edges) is $v + 2$.

Any pair of horizontal lines and any pair of vertical lines define a unique rectangle. Hence, the total number of rectangles formed is:
$$\text{Total rectangles} = \binom{h+2}{2} \times \binom{v+2}{2}$$

We are given that the total number of rectangles is 9:
$$\binom{h+2}{2} \times \binom{v+2}{2} = 9$$

Since $9 = 3 \times 3$, we have:
$$\binom{h+2}{2} = 3 \implies \frac{(h+2)(h+1)}{2} = 3 \implies h+2 = 3 \implies h = 1$$
$$\binom{v+2}{2} = 3 \implies v = 1$$

Drawing 1 horizontal line and 1 vertical line creates a $2 \times 2$ grid inside the rectangle, which yields exactly:
- 4 small rectangles of size $1 \times 1$
- 2 horizontal rectangles of size $2 \times 1$
- 2 vertical rectangles of size $1 \times 2$
- 1 large outer rectangle of size $2 \times 2$
- Total $= 4 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 9$ rectangles.

Total internal straight lines required $= h + v = 1 + 1 = \mathbf{2}$ lines.

Therefore, the minimum number of straight lines needed is **2** (Option 2).

### Question 9 (QID: 703509 | Part A)

**Question:**
A metro train runs at a speed of 45 km/h. It covers a total distance of 72 km on its route from origin to destination station in 2 hours that includes stops at intermediate stations. If it stops for 2 minutes at each station, how many intermediate stations are there in the route?

**Options:**
- 1. 24
- 2. 18
- 3. 12
- 4. 9

**Official Answer:**
**Option 3 (12)**

**Explanation:**
1. **Actual Running Time of the Train:**

2. **Total Scheduled Journey Time:**
   $$\text{Total time} = 2\text{ hours} = 120\text{ minutes}$$

3. **Total Stoppage Time:**
   $$\text{Stoppage time} = 120\text{ min} - 96\text{ min} = 24\text{ minutes}$$

4. **Number of Intermediate Stations:**
   Since the train halts for 2 minutes at each intermediate station:
   $$\text{Number of stations} = \frac{24\text{ minutes}}{2\text{ minutes/station}} = 12\text{ intermediate stations}$$

Therefore, there are **12** intermediate stations (Option 3).

### Question 14 (QID: 703514 | Part A)

**Question:**
If the sum of the two-digit numbers formed from the digits $a$ and $b$ is a perfect square, the sum of the digits is

**Options:**

**Official Answer:**
**Option 4 (11)**

The two-digit numbers formed using digits $a$ and $b$ are:
- Number 1: $10a + b$
- Number 2: $10b + a$

Their sum is:
$$\text{Sum} = (10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11a + 11b = 11(a + b)$$

For $11(a + b)$ to be a perfect square, since 11 is a prime number, $(a + b)$ must contain at least one factor of 11.
Since $a$ and $b$ are digits from 1 to 9, their sum lies in the range:
$$2 \le a + b \le 18$$

The only multiple of 11 in this range is:
$$a + b = 11$$

With $a + b = 11$, the sum of the numbers is $11 \times 11 = 121 = 11^2$, which is indeed a perfect square (for example, with digits 2 and 9: $29 + 92 = 121$; or with 3 and 8: $38 + 83 = 121$).

Therefore, the sum of the digits is **11** (Option 4).

### Question 16 (QID: 703516 | Part A)

- Failed Q2: $120 - 95 = 25$
- Failed Q3: $120 - 90 = 30$

$$\text{Min solving all four} = N - 115 = 120 - 115 = \mathbf{5}$$

Therefore, the smallest possible number of students that solved all four questions is **5** (Option 2).

### Question 17 (QID: 703517 | Part A)

Rivers A and B with constant water speeds $a$ and $b$ merge at point P to form river C with a constant water speed $c$.

A boat having speed $s$ in still water would reach point R from Q only if $s$ is more than

One gram of a polysaccharide composed of 1000 glucose units has the same effect on osmolarity as that of

**Options:**

- 2. 100 mg glucose

- 4. 1000 mg glucose

**Official Answer:**
**Option 1 (1 mg glucose)**

**Explanation:**
Osmolarity is a colligative property that depends strictly on the **number of solute particles (moles of particles)** per unit volume, irrespective of their molecular mass:

1. Let the molecular weight of a single glucose unit be $M_g$.
2. A polysaccharide composed of $N = 1000$ glucose units has a molecular weight approximately equal to:
   $$M_{\text{poly}} = 1000 \times M_g$$
3. One gram (1 g) of this polysaccharide corresponds to:
   $$n_{\text{poly}} = \frac{1\text{ g}}{M_{\text{poly}}} = \frac{1\text{ g}}{1000 \times M_g} = \frac{10^{-3}\text{ g}}{M_g} = \frac{1\text{ mg}}{M_g}\text{ moles}$$

   $$\text{Mass of glucose} = n_{\text{poly}} \times M_g = \left(\frac{1\text{ mg}}{M_g}\right) \times M_g = \mathbf{1\text{ mg}}$$

### Question 49 (QID: 703549 | Part B)

**Question:**
A plant heterozygous for a dominant trait was selfed. The progeny had 140 plants showing the dominant trait and 20 plants showing the recessive trait. A researcher hypothesised that there are two genes with identical functions that control the dominant trait. The researcher also proposed that the two genes are not linked. The researcher carried out a chi-square test to test the hypothesis. Which one of the following options is the correct chi-square value (rounded to second decimal) obtained by the researcher?

1. 22.86
2. 13.33
3. 10.67
4. 0.57

**Options:**
- 1. 22.86
- 2. 13.33
- 3. 10.67
- 4. 0.57

**Official Answer:**
**Option 3 (10.67)**

**Explanation:**
Let us compute the chi-square statistic ($\chi^2$) step-by-step:

1. **Hypothesis:** Two unlinked genes with identical functions (duplicate dominant epistasis) control the trait. In a dihybrid cross ($AaBb \times AaBb$), duplicate dominant epistasis produces a theoretical phenotypic ratio of **15 (dominant) : 1 (recessive)**.
2. **Observed counts ($O$):**
   - Dominant phenotype $= 140$
   - Recessive phenotype $= 20$
   - Total progeny $N = 140 + 20 = 160$
3. **Expected counts ($E$) under the 15:1 ratio:**
   - $E_{\text{dominant}} = \frac{15}{16} \times 160 = 150$
   - $E_{\text{recessive}} = \frac{1}{16} \times 160 = 10$
4. **Chi-square calculation:**
   $$\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$$
