### Question 21 (Client QID: 703221 | Part B)

**Question:**
The base composition of the genome of a newly identified virus is given below:
Adenine: 25%; Cytosine: 35%; Guanine: 30%; Thymidine: 10%

Based on this information, the genome of this virus is:

**Options:**
1. double-stranded DNA.
2. single-stranded DNA.
3. double-stranded RNA.
4. single-stranded RNA.

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 2 (single-stranded DNA)

**Detailed Explanation:**
According to Chargaff's rules for double-stranded DNA, the molar ratio of Adenine equals Thymine ($A = T$) and Guanine equals Cytosine ($G = C$). Here, the virus contains Thymidine ($10\%$), confirming it is a DNA virus (not RNA, which would contain Uracil). However, $A (25\%) \ne T (10\%)$ and $G (30\%) \ne C (35\%)$. The deviation from Chargaff's equivalence indicates that the bases are not paired in a complementary duplex, proving the genome is **single-stranded DNA** (ssDNA).

### Question 22 (Client QID: 703222 | Part B)

**Question:**
The amount of energy required to break a single covalent bond is:

**Options:**
1. 200–500 kJ/mol
2. 80–150 kJ/mol
3. 600–900 kJ/mol
4. 20–70 kJ/mol

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 1 and 2 (200–500 kJ/mol / 80–150 kJ/mol)

**Detailed Explanation:**
Typical single covalent bond energies in biological molecules (such as $\text{C}-\text{C}$, $\text{C}-\text{H}$, $\text{C}-\text{O}$, $\text{C}-\text{N}$, $\text{O}-\text{H}$) generally range between $300\text{ kJ/mol}$ and $460\text{ kJ/mol}$ ($50\text{ to }110\text{ kcal/mol}$), falling squarely within the $200\text{--}500\text{ kJ/mol}$ range. Some single covalent bonds with weaker overlap (e.g. $\text{O}-\text{O}$ peroxide, $\text{S}-\text{S}$ disulfide, or weak single bonds) have dissociation energies in the $150\text{--}250\text{ kJ/mol}$ range or below in solution. Because of varying standard reference tables, NTA officially accepted both **Option 1 (200–500 kJ/mol)** and **Option 2 (80–150 kJ/mol)**.

### Question 23 (Client QID: 703223 | Part B)

**Question:**
Which one of the following options represents parts of a tRNA that are involved in amino acid charging and interaction with the mRNA, respectively?

**Options:**
1. 5' end and D-loop
2. 3' end and T-loop
3. 5' end and anti-codon loop
4. 3' end and anti-codon loop

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 4 (3' end and anti-codon loop)

**Detailed Explanation:**
In transfer RNA (tRNA):
- Amino acid charging (aminoacylation) occurs at the conserved universal **3' CCA terminus**, specifically esterifying the carboxyl group of the cognate amino acid to the $2'$- or $3'$-OH group of the terminal adenosine residue catalyzed by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases.
- Decoding of genetic information occurs via base pairing between the three-nucleotide **anticodon loop** of the tRNA and the complementary triplet codon of the mRNA within the ribosomal decoding center (A site).
Thus, the parts are the 3' end and anti-codon loop respectively.

### Question 24 (Client QID: 703224 | Part B)

**Question:**
An enzyme "X" converts a L-amino acid to a racemic mixture of D- and L- forms. Which one of the following coenzymes is utilized by enzyme X for this conversion?

**Options:**
1. Pyridoxal phosphate
2. Thiamine pyrophosphate
3. Flavin adenine dinucleotide
4. Tetrahydrofolate

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 1 (Pyridoxal phosphate)

**Detailed Explanation:**
Amino acid racemases (such as alanine racemase) interconvert L- and D-amino acid stereoisomers. These enzymes utilize **Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP)**, the active coenzyme form of vitamin $\text{B}_6$. ### Question 71 (Client QID: 703271 | Part C)

**Question:**
If the length of a single continuous α-helical polypeptide is 108 Å, which one of the following statements is true?

**Options:**
1. The α-helix contains 72 amino acids without Proline(s) and Glycine(s)
2. The α-helix contains 76 amino acids without Alanine(s) and Valine(s)
3. The α-helix contains 80 amino acids without Lysine(s) and Aspartic Acid(s)
4. The α-helix contains 74 amino acids without Arginine(s) and Histidine(s)

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 1 (The α-helix contains 72 amino acids without Proline(s) and Glycine(s))

**Detailed Explanation:**
In a standard Pauling-Corey right-handed $\alpha$-helix:
- Axial translation per amino acid residue (rise per residue, $d$) is $1.5\text{ \AA}$ ($0.15\text{ nm}$).
- Number of residues in a helix of length $L = 108\text{ \AA}$ is:
  $$n = \frac{L}{d} = \frac{108\text{ \AA}}{1.5\text{ \AA/residue}} = 72\text{ residues}$$
- Proline lacks an amide hydrogen for i+4 backbone hydrogen bonding and causes steric clash due to its rigid pyrrolidine ring ('helix breaker'), while glycine has conformational flexibility that entropically destabilizes the helix.
Therefore, the $\alpha$-helix contains 72 amino acids without Proline and Glycine.

**Question:**
The melting curves shown below are of two double-stranded DNA molecules of the same length.

### Question 80 (Client QID: 703280 | Part C)

**Question:**
The critical micellar concentration (CMC) of a detergent is 5 mM. Choose the option that uses the minimum amount of detergent that can be used for cell lysis and is least likely to denature soluble proteins?

**Options:**
1. 0.5 mM
2. 10 mM
3. 6 mM
4. 2.5 mM

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 3 (6 mM)

**Detailed Explanation:**
Critical Micellar Concentration (CMC) principles in biochemical membrane solubilization:
1. Detergents dissolve phospholipid bilayers by partitioning into the membrane and forming mixed micelles with lipids and membrane proteins.
2. Membrane solubilization and efficient cell lysis occur only **at or slightly above the CMC**. Below the CMC (e.g. 0.5 mM or 2.5 mM), detergents exist solely as free monomers and cannot disrupt bilayers effectively.
3. Excessive concentrations of detergent (such as 10 mM) increase the risk of stripping structural boundary lipids and denaturing or inactivating soluble cytosolic and peripheral proteins.
4. Therefore, **6 mM** (just above the CMC of 5 mM) is the minimum concentration required to achieve complete membrane lysis while maintaining the native folded conformation of soluble proteins.

### Question 28 (Client QID: 703228 | Part B)

**Question:**
Which one of the following statements about biological membranes is INCORRECT?

**Options:**
1. Lipid molecules can diffuse freely in the plane of the bilayer.
2. The ratio of the size of head group to fatty acyl chain of phospholipids contributes to the curvature properties of lipid membranes.
3. Individual lipid molecules rapidly rotate along their long axis.
4. Phospholipids freely diffuse from one leaflet of bilayer to another.

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 4 (Phospholipids freely diffuse from one leaflet of bilayer to another.)

**Detailed Explanation:**
Membrane dynamics and phospholipid mobility:
- **Lateral diffusion:** Phospholipids diffuse very rapidly within the same plane/leaflet of the membrane (diffusion coefficient $D \approx 10^{-8}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$).
- **Rotational diffusion:** Lipids rapidly rotate around their long molecular axis.
- **Transverse movement (flip-flop):** Moving a polar/charged hydrophilic head group across the hydrophobic core of the bilayer has a massive thermodynamic activation energy barrier. Thus, statement 4 is INCORRECT.

**Question:**
Newly identified proteins X, Y, and Z are associated with endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane fraction. 2. B and C
3. A and C
4. B and D

Membrane protein extraction criteria:
- **Peripheral membrane proteins** are bound to membrane surfaces or integral proteins through non-covalent electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding. ### Question 87 (Client QID: 703287 | Part C)

**Question:**

A. Team I isolated the chromatin with low-ionic-strength buffer, and Team II isolated chromatin with an ionic strength of ~0.15M KCl (physiological ionic strength).

1. A only
2. B only
3. C and D
4. B and D

Higher-order chromatin compaction is exquisitely sensitive to monovalent and divalent salt concentration:
- At **low ionic strength** ($< 5\text{--}10\text{ mM monovalent salt}$, e.g. low-salt buffer), electrostatic repulsion between adjacent linker DNA segments forces chromatin to decondense into the extended 10-nm 'beads-on-a-string' conformation (nucleosome cores connected by linker DNA).

### Question 49 (Client QID: 703249 | Part B)

**Question:**
While developing genetic maps, Alfred Sturtevant proposed that genetic distances are additive. From test crosses involving two genes, if the genetic distance between genes A and B was observed to be 15 cM and between B and C was 10 cM, then the percentage of recombinants observed between A and C would be 25, given that the arrangement is A—B—C. **Options:**

2. Complete interference
3. Negative interference
4. Variable interference

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 2 (Complete interference)

Linkage mapping and genetic interference:
- For linked genes in order $A - B - C$:
  $$d_{AB} = 15\text{ cM} = 0.15, \quad d_{BC} = 10\text{ cM} = 0.10$$
- The observed recombinant frequency between the outer markers $A$ and $C$ ($RF_{AC}$) accounts only for single crossovers (SCO) because double crossovers (DCO) restore parental allele combinations for the outer markers:
  $$RF_{AC} = \text{SCO}_1 + \text{SCO}_2 = (d_{AB} - \text{DCO}) + (d_{BC} - \text{DCO}) = d_{AB} + d_{BC} - 2(\text{DCO})$$

- A coefficient of coincidence of 0 ($C = 0$), which corresponds to **Complete Interference ($I = 1 - C = 1$)**, completely prevents double crossovers from occurring.
Thus, additivity of observed recombination percentages holds only under **Complete interference**.

**Question:**
Which one of the following statements about the molecular clock hypothesis as proposed by Zuckerkandl and Pauling (1962) is CORRECT?

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 1 (DNA and protein sequences evolve at a relatively constant rate.)

**Detailed Explanation:**
The Molecular Clock Hypothesis:
- In 1962–1965, Emile Zuckerkandl and Linus Pauling analyzed hemoglobin polypeptide sequences from diverse animal lineages (e.g. human, horse, cow) whose divergence times had been established from paleontological fossil records.
- They observed that the number of amino acid differences between any two lineages was approximately proportional to the geological time elapsed since their common ancestor.
- Based on this empirical finding, they proposed the **Molecular Clock Hypothesis**, asserting that biological macromolecules (nucleotide sequences in DNA and amino acid sequences in proteins) accumulate evolutionary changes (substitutions) at an approximately constant rate over geological time.
- This constancy forms the mathematical foundation for estimating divergence times in molecular phylogenetics, later formalized theoretically under Motoo Kimura's Neutral Theory of Molecular Evolution (where substitution rate equals the neutral mutation rate, $k = \mu$).

- **Position i (\(T \to C \to A\) in Lineage 1, \(T\) in Lineage 2):** More than one substitution event has occurred consecutively at the same nucleotide site along a single lineage (first \(T \to C\), then \(C \to A\)). This is termed **multiple substitutions**.
- **Position ii (\(T \to C\) in Lineage 1, \(T \to C\) in Lineage 2):** The same ancestral nucleotide (\(T\)) has independently mutated to the identical derived state (\(C\)) in both diverging lineages. This is termed **parallel substitution**.
- **Position iii (\(A \to G \to C\) in Lineage 1, \(A \to C\) in Lineage 2):** Starting from ancestral \(A\), Lineage 1 evolved through an intermediate state \(G\) to \(C\), while Lineage 2 mutated directly to \(C\). Both lineages converge on the same final nucleotide state through different evolutionary pathways. This is termed **convergent substitution**.
- **Position iv (\(G \to A \to G\) in Lineage 1, \(G\) in Lineage 2):** A site mutates from ancestral \(G \to A\) and subsequently mutates back from \(A \to G\), restoring the ancestral state. This is termed **back substitution (reversion)**.
- Therefore, the correct matching is: **i) multiple substitutions, ii) parallel substitution, iii) convergent substitution, iv) back substitution** (Option 2).

**Detailed Explanation:**
Total Internal Reflection Fluorescence (TIRF) microscopy for single-molecule detection:
- In standard epifluorescence or confocal microscopy, background fluorescence and Rayleigh scattering from out-of-focus fluorophores in the thick aqueous sample obscure weak emissions from individual fluorophores.
- **TIRF microscopy** solves this by directing excitation laser light through a high-refractive-index coverslip (glass, \(n_1 \approx 1.52\)) toward an aqueous medium (water/buffer, \(n_2 \approx 1.33\)) at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle (\(\theta_c = \arcsin(n_2/n_1)\)).

- This ultra-thin optical sectioning restricts excitation exclusively to fluorophores within \(\sim 100\text{ nm}\) of the coverglass (e.g. membrane-bound proteins, receptor-ligand complexes), eliminating background fluorescence from the bulk solution and achieving the exceptionally high signal-to-noise ratio needed to image and track **single fluorescent molecules**.

**Question:**
Using an analytical ultracentrifugation sedimentation velocity run, a researcher has calculated the S value (Svedberg units) of a part of the freshly purified 12 kDa enzyme to be ~ 1.83, corresponding to its monomeric state. However, during an identical run of the remaining protein after 2 days, the researcher finds that the S value increased to ~ 2.57. What would be the correct conclusion about the enzyme in question?

**Options:**
1. The enzyme is aggregated and forms a homo octamer.
2. The enzyme is monomeric but is now associated with the buffer components.
3. The enzyme has undergone monomer to a homodimer transition.
4. The enzyme has degraded due to autocatalytic activity.

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 3 (The enzyme has undergone monomer to a homodimer transition.)

**Detailed Explanation:**
Analytical Ultracentrifugation (AUC) and oligomeric transitions:
- In sedimentation velocity AUC, the sedimentation coefficient \(s\) (in Svedberg units, \(10^{-13}\text{ s}\)) is described by the Svedberg equation:
  $$s = \frac{M(1 - \bar{v}\rho)}{N_A f}$$
  where \(M\) is molecular mass, \(\bar{v}\) is partial specific volume, \(\rho\) is solvent density, and \(f\) is the frictional coefficient.
- For compact globular proteins of similar shape, the frictional coefficient scales as \(f \propto R_H \propto M^{1/3}\). Consequently, the sedimentation coefficient scales with molecular mass as:
  $$s \propto \frac{M}{M^{1/3}} = M^{2/3}$$
- For a monomer-to-homodimer transition where mass doubles (\(M_{\text{dimer}} = 2 M_{\text{monomer}}\)):
  $$\frac{s_{\text{dimer}}}{s_{\text{monomer}}} \approx 2^{2/3} \approx 1.587$$
- Accounting for slight hydrodynamic elongation/asymmetry, typical monomer-to-dimer transitions exhibit an \(s\)-value ratio between 1.4 and 1.6.
- For the enzyme in question:
  $$\frac{s_2}{s_1} = \frac{2.57}{1.83} \approx 1.404$$
  which corresponds to a **monomer-to-homodimer transition**.
- If it had formed an octamer, the mass would increase 8-fold and \(s \approx 1.83 \times 8^{2/3} = 1.83 \times 4 = 7.32\text{ S}\). If it had degraded, \(s\) would decrease.

The following 15 observations are heights (in inches) of 15 persons:

65, 61, 63, 65, 61, 61, 60, 60, 65, 85, 65, 86, 61, 65, 62

Which of the following is true?

**Options:**
1. Mean < Median < Mode
2. Median < Mode < Mean
3. Mode < Median < Mean
4. Median < Mean < Mode

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 2 (Median < Mode < Mean)

**Detailed Explanation:**
Arrange the 15 observations in ascending order:
$$60, 60, 61, 61, 61, 61, 62, 63, 65, 65, 65, 65, 65, 85, 86$$
- **Mode:** The value 65 occurs 5 times (highest frequency) $\implies \text{Mode} = 65$.
- **Median:** For $n = 15$, the median is the 8th term in sorted order $\implies \text{Median} = 63$.
- **Mean:**
  $$\sum x = 60(2) + 61(4) + 62(1) + 63(1) + 65(5) + 85(1) + 86(1)$$
  $$\sum x = 120 + 244 + 62 + 63 + 325 + 85 + 86 = 985$$
  $$\text{Mean} = \frac{985}{15} \approx 65.67$$
Comparing the three statistical measures:
$$\text{Median (63)} < \text{Mode (65)} < \text{Mean (65.67)}$$
This matches Option 2: Median < Mode < Mean.

### Question 20 (Client QID: 703220 | Part A)

**Question:**
One letter is picked at random from each of the words LUNAR and LANDER. The chance that the letters picked from both words belong to the word PRAGYAN is

**Options:**
1. 3/10
2. 3/7
3. 1/2
4. 7/10

**Official NTA Answer:** Option 1 (3/10)

**Detailed Explanation:**
The reference target word is **PRAGYAN**, whose unique letters are:
$$\{\text{P, R, A, G, Y, N}\}$$
- **Word 1: LUNAR** (5 letters: L, U, N, A, R)
  - Letters that belong to PRAGYAN: N, A, R (3 letters).
  - Probability $P_1 = \frac{3}{5}$.
- **Word 2: LANDER** (6 letters: L, A, N, D, E, R)
  - Letters that belong to PRAGYAN: A, N, R (3 letters).
  - Probability $P_2 = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$.
- Because the selections are independent:
  $$P(\text{both letters belong to PRAGYAN}) = P_1 \times P_2 = \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{10}$$
This matches Option 1.
