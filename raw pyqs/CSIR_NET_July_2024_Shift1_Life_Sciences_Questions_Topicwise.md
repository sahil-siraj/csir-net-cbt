- **Question:** In humans, if both parents are of blood group AB, then, there is a 1/4 probability that their offspring will be of blood group A, and 1/2 probability that the offspring will be of blood group AB. If the couple have three children, what is the probability that NONE of the children will be of blood group A or AB?
  1. 1/4 | 2. 3/4 | 3. 1/64 | 4. 63/64
- **Correct Answer:** **Option 3 (1/64)**
- **Explanation:** Parents are $I^A I^B \times I^A I^B$. Offspring possibilities: $1/4\ I^A I^A$ (A), $1/2\ I^A I^B$ (AB), and $1/4\ I^B I^B$ (B). The probability that a child is NOT A or AB equals the probability of being blood group B, which is $1/4$. For all 3 children to be neither A nor AB: $(1/4)^3 = 1/64$.

#### Sr 8 | Question ID: `703508`
- **Question:** Consider the first few consecutive natural numbers. The ratio of the sum of their squares to their sum can NEVER be:
  1. 67 | 2. 75 | 3. 91 | 4. 100
- **Correct Answer:** **Option 4 (100)**
- **Explanation:** The ratio for the first $n$ natural numbers is $\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^n i} = \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)/6}{n(n+1)/2} = \frac{2n+1}{3}$. Setting $\frac{2n+1}{3} = k \implies 2n+1 = 3k \implies 2n = 3k - 1$. Since $2n$ is even, $3k - 1$ must be even, so $3k$ must be odd, which requires $k$ to be an odd integer. Among the choices, 67, 75, and 91 are odd integers (yielding $n = 100, 112, 136$ respectively), whereas 100 is even ($2n = 299 \implies n = 149.5$, not an integer). Hence, the ratio can never be 100.
